City of Toronto · RentSafeTO · in effect since June 15, 2026

The City now inspects your building — and posts the result on your door.

RentSafeTO covers ~3,600 rental buildings (3+ storeys, 10+ units). Each is evaluated every two years against 50 scored categories, and since June 15 must display a green, yellow, or red sign at its entrance.

How the City runs it

Municipal Code Ch. 354 & 629

  • Evaluated every two years; scheduled so you can unlock common areas.
  • The officer walks interior and exterior, records notes and photos.
  • Health or life-safety issues get an order on the spot.
  • The colour is refreshed daily as orders are issued or cleared.

What you're on the hook for

Owner & operator obligations

  • Post the sign at the entrance; replace within 14 days of a colour change.
  • Disclose the rating to every prospective tenant, and yearly to existing ones.
  • Post the full evaluation on the Tenant Notification Board.
  • Keep a mandatory, scored 5-year State of Good Repair (Capital) Plan.
The details — scoring, enforcement & the myths

How the score is built

Proactive score−Reactive deductions=Total score
50 categories
Each scored 1–3, weighted by risk. 17 high-risk categories carry 51% — most are envelope, balcony and structure.
−1% / −2%
Each Order/Notice costs 1% for 30 days; an Emergency Order 2% for four months. They revert when fixed.
Daily refresh
Percentile-ranked and updated daily — your colour can slip purely from open orders.
Audit

Lowest 2.5% get a full audit — every common area, garage, grounds, plus a door-to-door tenant canvass.

Targeted engagement

Score drops 5%+ for two months, or red for two months → the City sets up on your property and talks to tenants.

Orders & re-inspection

Orders posted on the board; officer re-inspects ~5 days after the deadline.

Straight talk — what the colour does and doesn't do

It does NOT

Ban you from leasing or raising rent. The colour is a maintenance signal, not a closure. Rent stays provincial under the RTA.

It DOES

Put a public sign on your door, force disclosure to every prospective tenant, and escalate City attention. An open unit order can block that unit until cleared.

Why the reserve has to be bigger than the work. A real reserve leaves room for the things a plan can't schedule — a fire, a sudden roof leak, a structural surprise. If your reserve sits within ~1.5× of what you've actually committed to contracts, it isn't a reserve — double it.
