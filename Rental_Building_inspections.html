City of Toronto · RentSafeTO · in effect since June 15, 2026

The City now inspects your building on a schedule — and posts the result on your door.

RentSafeTO covers roughly 3,600 purpose-built rental buildings (three or more storeys, ten or more units). Every one is evaluated on a two-year cycle against 50 scored categories, and since June 15 every one must display a green, yellow, or red sign at its main entrance.

How the City runs the inspection

Municipal Code Ch. 354 & 629 · bylaw enforcement officer

  • Every building is evaluated once every two years; you're notified in advance and the visit is scheduled so you or a designate can unlock common areas and amenities.
  • The officer walks the interior and the exterior, records notes and photographs, and checks the building against the property standards.
  • Any health or life-safety issue gets an Order to Comply or Notice of Violation issued on the spot.
  • The score is calculated, delivered through the online portal, and the colour is refreshed daily as orders are issued or cleared.

What you're now on the hook for

Owner & operator obligations

  • Post the coloured sign at the main entrance, and replace it within 14 days of any colour change.
  • Disclose the rating to every prospective tenant before and after signing — and to existing tenants once a year.
  • Post the full evaluation result on the Tenant Notification Board and provide the rating to anyone who asks.
  • Maintain a mandatory, scored State of Good Repair (Capital) Plan — a five-year forecast of major repairs.

How the score is actually built

Proactive score − Reactive deductions = Total building score
50 categories
Each scored 1–3 and weighted by risk: 17 high-risk categories carry 51% of the total — and most of them are envelope, balcony and structure.
−1% / −2%
Every Order or Notice knocks off 1% for 30 days; an Emergency Order, 2% for four months. They revert when you fix the issue.
Daily refresh
Scores are percentile-ranked against other buildings and updated daily — so your colour can slip between evaluations purely from open orders.

5-YEAR CAPITAL PLAN

The plan the City requires is the plan we produce.

Every owner must keep a State of Good Repair (Capital) Plan — a five-year forecast covering roof, façade, windows, balcony guards, the underground garage, mechanical systems and more, on the City's template, handed to any tenant within 60 days of request. That five-year plan of prioritized, specified, costed work is exactly the output of a Tenera inspection. We hand you a compliance document and a repair roadmap in one.

What a low score sets in motion

Audit

Buildings in the lowest 2.5% are prioritized for a full audit — every common area, the garage, the grounds, plus a door-to-door tenant canvass. 30 days' notice, posted on the board. Fees apply.

Targeted engagement

If the score drops 5%+ for two months running, or the sign stays red for two months, the City sets up an information booth on your property and talks to your tenants directly.

Orders & re-inspection

Orders arrive by registered mail and get posted on the board; the officer re-inspects about five days after the deadline. Appeals are possible but slow the actual fix.

Straight talk — what the colour does and doesn't do

It does NOT

A yellow or red rating does not ban you from leasing units or raising rent. The colour is a maintenance signal, not a closure. Rent increases stay a provincial matter under the Residential Tenancies Act — the City has no say.

It DOES

It puts a public sign on your door, forces disclosure to every prospective tenant, and escalates City attention. And an open order on a unit — or a vital-service or pest issue — can stop you renting that unit until it's cleared. We keep you clear of both.
